# The Region Map is a 16:9 painting with code-drawn Stage Markers

The Campaign screen shows one Region Map at a time: a flat 2D painting in landscape 16:9 that fits the screen with no scrolling. The painting shows the land, the Trail and an empty clearing for each Stage. The game draws the Stage Markers, the progress line, the Region name and the Star chests on top of it. Each Stage Marker is a React Aria button. Each Region Map has its own clearing positions and road path, measured on its painting (`apps/web/src/features/campaign/region-map.ts`).

A Region with no painting yet shows a placeholder Region Map, so that the Player can play its Stages before the art exists (ADR-0034). The placeholder is a calm sage ground with a soft road under the Trail, and no landmarks. It uses the clearing positions and the road path of Hearthvale (`shownRegionMap`). A Region with no Stages in the data shows only the ground, with no Trail and no Stage Markers, and its Region banner says that it comes later (`regionStatus`). The placeholder of a Region goes away when that Region gets its entry in `REGION_MAPS`.

We did this because the game is landscape, desktop first and also on phones in landscape, and the Town and the Battle Painting already use a 16:9 painting with a 1600 × 900 code box (web ADR-0004, web ADR-0006). 10 Stage Markers fit on one screen. The state of a Stage (Done, Open, Locked, best Stars) changes with the progress of the Player, and the labels need text in each language, so the art cannot contain them.

## Considered Options

- **A tall portrait map with vertical scrolling, as in many mobile map games.** Rejected. It does not fit a landscape screen, and the Player must scroll to find the next Stage.
- **A map wider than the screen, with panning.** Rejected. 10 Stages do not need the space, and panning adds input code for mouse, touch and keyboard.
- **Paint the markers and the dashed line into the art.** Rejected. The art then cannot show which Stages are done or locked, and each change of a Region needs a new painting.
- **Hide a Region with no painting, or show a "coming soon" card.** Rejected. The Stages of Region 2 exist before its painting, and the Player must be able to play them. The placeholder lets the Stages, the Region rules and the art move at different speeds.
- **Show the Hearthvale painting for a Region with no painting.** Rejected. Its landmarks (Brassbelly Hall and the Stage landmarks) are wrong for the other Regions, and the Player would think that they are back in Hearthvale.
- **Always cover the full screen with the painting, under the Town Bar too.** First chosen, then changed. On a browser window wider than 16:9 (for example 1916 × 967), the cover size cut the top of the Boss landmark and hid the bottom of the painting under the Town Bar: the Player saw only 82% of its height. A stretch of at most 10% on the x axis keeps the art true enough, and the Player sees about 91% of the height on that window.
- **Fit all of the painting on the screen, with a soft copy of it at the sides.** Tried, then rejected. With a stretch of at most 10%, the blurred bands at the sides were wide and looked like an error, and the painting and the Stage Markers got smaller.
- **Cover the screen width above the Town Bar, with a stretch of at most 10%.** First chosen, then changed. On a browser window wider than 16:9 (for example 1917 × 960), the screen cut about 100 px of the painting at the top and the bottom, so the Boss landmark and the bottom of the painting were not visible. The full height of the painting is more important than a stretch of less than 20%.
- **One set of clearing positions for all Region Maps.** First chosen, then changed. The generated Hearthvale painting put its clearings higher than the planned positions, with the Boss clearing in the top band. Inpainting each map to fixed positions costs more than one list of positions for each map, and the frame code below checks each list on each screen shape.

## Consequences

- The positions in 12 — Region Concepts 1.1 are a target for the artist, not a fixed rule. After a painting is final, its clearing centers and the center line of its road are measured by hand and kept as its Region Map data.
- The screen does not crop the top or the bottom of the painting. `mapFrame` fits the full height of the painting above the Town Bar. It stretches the painting on the x axis by at most 20% (`FIT_STRETCH`), or squashes it by at most 10% (`MAX_STRETCH`), so that the painting fills the screen width. On a 16:9 screen, the Player sees all of the painting above the Town Bar, with an 8% stretch. On a screen that is wider than this lets (a 21:9 screen, or a browser window a little wider than 2.1:1), a soft copy of the same painting fills the sides. On a screen that is narrower (a 4:3 screen), the screen cuts the sides of the painting. On a phone, the painting gets larger when the Stage Markers need the room to stay apart, and the screen then cuts the painting. `mapFrame` keeps every Stage Marker on the screen above the Town Bar, and keeps the markers clear of the Region banner and the Star chest plate. It also keeps at least half of the Boss landmark on the screen, so that the Player sees where the Trail goes. When the screen cannot hold the Stage Markers (a short screen), the painting gets smaller. Unit tests check this for 4:3 to 21:9, for a wide browser window and for phones in landscape. On desktop and laptop screens, they check that the full height of the painting is above the Town Bar.
- The progress line follows a path that is traced by hand from each painting.
- A change to the number of Stages in a Region, or to the screen layout, needs new positions and maybe new paintings.
- On the placeholder, the plain ground fills the sides of the screen, not a soft copy of a painting. The placeholder road is a wide, faint line on the Hearthvale legs, so the progress line does not float on the plain ground.
- The Hearthvale positions are only for the placeholder. When a Region gets its painting, its clearing centers and road path are measured on that painting (12 — Region Concepts, step 6), and the Stage Markers move to them. The Stage data does not change.
